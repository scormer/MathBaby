Personalized Math / Logic Learning App

Full product-inception research handoff derived from the app-development discussion. The current direction is a parent-facing, low-friction personalized learning system for children's mathematics and mathematical thinking. It maintains a stable but extensible capability model, infers capability status from sparse parent check-ins and optional app signals, and recommends playful activities without requiring parents to assess every activity.

Current Canonical Decisions

These supersede earlier, more assessment-heavy proposals.
Parent burden is a hard constraint.

No required feedback after every activity. Parent interaction should be sparse, episodic and skippable.

Observation is detached from activities.

Activities are learning experiences. Parent check-ins are a separate signal channel.

Questionnaire first.

Default parent input is behavior-based questions using YES / NO / NOT SURE.

Direct capability inference is acceptable.

For MVP, questionnaire state can update CapabilityStatus through AI without exposing an evidence workflow.

Capability is broader than math content.

Top families: Math Content, Mathematical Thinking, Metacognition.

No Communication/Social capability family.

Teach-back, parent debrief and error critique are activity mechanisms used to strengthen math thinking/metacognition.

Capability graph is stable but open.

Stable core for longitudinal tracking; experimental perimeter can evolve under version control.

Graph, matrix and tree have different jobs.

Graph for meaning; matrix for mappings/computation; tree only for browsing/UI.

Jev is a simple observation-to-capability classifier.

For the generic 43-capability test, use one flat yes/no (noul) question per Capability and treat P(true) as that observation’s positive-support percentage. Keep learner history, graph logic and final policy in code.

Full LLM use should be selective.

Optional free text, reports and offline content/ontology work—not every tap.

Simple UX
Parent answers a few questions → AI updates capability map → app recommends next activities.
Decisions changed through v1.2
  • Capability Model v0.1 now contains 43 concrete draft Capabilities.
  • Formal boundary frozen: Capability ≠ Skill ≠ Indicator ≠ Evidence ≠ Activity.
  • Skills get one owner Capability; Evidence may support multiple Capabilities.
  • The top-level learner model is now a sparse-evidence Capability Belief Model; classical BKT is deferred to future dense skill telemetry.
  • Capability DAG is a prior/readiness structure. Direct evidence can override missing predecessor observations.
  • Jev simplification (v1.2): the generic observation classifier is a flat set of 43 independent yes/no (noul) Capability questions. Use P(true) as the observation’s positive-support percentage. Do not use SUPPORTS/CONTRADICTS/MIXED/INSUFFICIENT labels in the generic runtime schema.
  • A low Jev support percentage means the observation does not positively support that Capability; it does not by itself mean the child lacks the Capability.
1

Product Thesis and Opportunity

The opportunity is not another adaptive worksheet app. The stronger product is a high-resolution mathematical development map that helps parents understand how their child thinks, what is developing, and what playful challenge is useful next.

Positioning direction: Understand how your child thinks mathematically, then give them the right playful challenge next.

The app should eventually distinguish reciting number words vs enumerating objects; cardinality vs recounting; quantity comparison with objects vs numerals; count-all vs count-on vs decomposition; pattern continuation vs identifying the repeat unit; solving one example vs transferring structure; and using a strategy vs noticing/choosing it.

Commercial hypothesis
The strongest differentiated promise is parent-visible insight into mathematical development and thinking, not generic “personalized math.”
2

Research Foundation

No single mature, commercially reusable framework was found that simultaneously supplies ages 3–10, granular constructs, developmental sequencing, prerequisites, misconceptions, evidence definitions and activity recommendations.

Developmental backbone

Clements & Sarama Learning Trajectories are the closest conceptual foundation for early mathematics: learning goals, developmental levels of thinking and activities. They are especially relevant through roughly age 8. Treat them as research input, not a database to copy.

Standards

Ontario, Common Core, UK, Australia, Singapore and similar standards are coverage/reporting layers. They should map onto the app model, not define it.

Learning-science mechanisms already identified

  • guided play;
  • worked examples and guidance fading;
  • retrieval and spacing;
  • interleaving where discrimination matters;
  • self-explanation;
  • analogical comparison / structure mapping;
  • erroneous examples / error critique;
  • metacognitive planning, monitoring and review;
  • multiple representations;
  • cognitive-load/task-confound awareness.
Research implication
Developmental trajectories are common paths and priors, not hard age locks or universal stage laws.
3

Scope, Users and Product Boundary

Architect for roughly ages 3–10, but initial focus should likely be 4–8. Around age 3, language, attention, motor control, task familiarity and adult assistance strongly confound direct assessment. A parent-assisted preschool mode can be added later. Ages 8–10 can extend into multidigit reasoning, multiplication/division, fractions, decimals, area/volume, data and complex problem solving.

ActorPrimary need
ChildPlay, agency, success, challenge, story, discovery, pattern/strategy awareness.
ParentLow burden, trustworthy explanation, strengths/gaps, what to do next, worthwhile screen time.

Scope discipline: do not become a broad communication/social-development app. Explaining, teaching back and critiquing another character are learning mechanics.

Capability Model v0.1 — First Draft (43 Capabilities)

Actual ontology content, not only design criteria. Status: DRAFT for pressure-testing.
Capability = a relatively durable, transferable capacity to understand mathematical content, reason mathematically, or regulate one’s own mathematical learning across a family of situations.
Core boundary rule
Capabilities are the stable things the product cares about in the child. Skills are narrower instructional components. Indicators define what a parent could plausibly notice. Evidence is the actual dated answer/observation.

Three Capability families

FamilyPurposeCount in v0.1
MATH_CONTENTWhat mathematical structures/concepts the child understands and can use.34
MATH_THINKINGHow the child reasons across mathematical content: model, plan, generalize, justify, transfer.5
METACOGNITIONHow the child monitors and regulates their own mathematical thinking/learning.4

Semantic exclusivity is the goal; evidence exclusivity is not. One parent observation may legitimately support several distinct Capabilities. For example, “She made 10 to solve 8+5 and explained why” may support AB-02, MT-03, and MC-02. The constructs remain distinct even when evidence overlaps.

Capability-vs-Skill test

  • If the construct remains meaningful when numbers, objects, representations, and activity format change, it may be a Capability.
  • If it is one procedure, strategy, developmental milestone, or narrow knowledge component, it is usually a Skill.
  • Number ranges such as “count to 10/20/50,” set size, layout, distractors, and similar challenge variables are normally difficulty parameters, not new Capabilities.
  • Each Skill should have exactly one owner Capability for taxonomy cleanliness, while it may SUPPORT other Capabilities.

These definitions are foundational and should remain stable across implementation changes.

Why Indicator and Evidence must be separate

Indicator = what would count as useful observable information. Evidence = what actually happened this time. Indicators are versioned ontology/content objects; Evidence is runtime history.

Why Activity stays separate from Evidence

Activities are primarily for learning. Parents are not required to report after them. An activity may generate optional telemetry/evidence, but the capability model must also work when no activity feedback exists.

Skill ownership

Skill
  owner_capability_id   # exactly one for classification
  supports_capability_ids[]  # zero or more cross-links
  difficulty_parameters{}
  representation_tags[]
  strategy_tags[]

Example: MAKE_TEN is owned by AB-02. It may also support NQ-05 and produce evidence relevant to MT-03, but it is not duplicated under those branches.

Evidence mapping rule

One Evidence event may map to several Capabilities with different strengths/confidences. This is desirable because human behavior is multi-informative. The ontology stays exclusive by definition, not by forcing every event into one bucket.

Maintaining the Capability DAG-ish Graph

The whole model is a graph; only prerequisite-like edges need acyclicity.

Capability Graph
  ├── REQUIRES          # must have no cycles
  ├── FACILITATES
  ├── SUPPORTS
  ├── TRANSFER_RELATED
  ├── PART_OF
  └── RELATED_TO

Use stable immutable IDs, lifecycle states and graph versions. Version edges with rationale, confidence/evidence grade and introduced/deprecated versions. Never allow production correlations to automatically rewrite prerequisites.

Metacognition, Abstraction and Higher-Level Thinking

Structure discovery

same structure / same surface
→ same structure / different objects
→ different story
→ different representation
→ near contrast
→ counterexample
→ child generates another example

Use prompts such as “What is the same?”, “What changed?”, “What stayed true?” to help children extract relational structure rather than only solve instances.

Visual indexing

Use a consistent relational visual grammar—part-whole, number line, equal groups, arrays, balance/comparison—then fade support and vary representation so visuals remain retrieval scaffolds, not shortcuts.

Metacognitive progression

Early: notice/label (“You tried counting.”). Middle: compare strategies. Later: plan/monitor/evaluate. Advanced: generalize and evaluate when strategies do or do not work.

Teach-back and review

“Tell me what you did/learned” is an activity mode that forces retrieval, organization, strategy awareness and summary. Parent follow-up can trigger another thinking cycle without creating a new communication-capability domain.

Error critique

“Milo says 2+3=4. Is he right?” can exercise content knowledge, plausibility checking, error detection and metacognition. Erroneous examples should be used when prior knowledge is sufficient.

  • Do not ask a question whose answer will not affect the model or recommendation.
  • Batch questions rather than interrupt repeatedly.
  • Allow skip.
  • Avoid fake precision.

Capability Belief Model: Sparse Evidence, Bayesian Thinking and BKT Position

The app’s data-generating process is intentionally sparse and irregular: parent questionnaire answers, optional free text, occasional activity feedback, and potentially some future structured app telemetry. Therefore classical Bayesian Knowledge Tracing (BKT) should not be the v1 core learner model.

Why classical BKT is not the primary fit

  • BKT works best with repeated sequential opportunities tagged to a known skill/KC.
  • Your parent feedback is optional, asynchronous, heterogeneous, and often capability-level rather than attempt-level.
  • BKT itself does not require a strict prerequisite tree; the main mismatch is sparse/irregular evidence and uncertain skill attribution.
  • Forcing questionnaire/free-text observations into pseudo-attempt sequences would add complexity without improving parent UX.

Recommended v1 state

CapabilityBelief
  capability_id

  P_UNKNOWN
  P_NOT_YET
  P_DEVELOPING
  P_CONSISTENT

  displayed_status       # argmax/policy-derived descriptive state
  confidence
  last_evidence_at
  staleness
  evidence_count
  ontology_version

Parent-facing vocabulary remains:

UNKNOWN       # insufficient or conflicting information
NOT_YET       # meaningful difficulty is indicated
DEVELOPING    # partial / inconsistent / emerging
CONSISTENT    # usually demonstrated in expected situations

Bayesian principle without textbook BKT

Use Bayesian thinking: new evidence modifies belief about a Capability. Initially this can be implemented as calibrated weighted/probabilistic updates rather than a complex Bayesian network.

P(CapabilityState | Evidence)
  ∝ P(Evidence | CapabilityState)
    × P(CapabilityState before evidence)

Different sources can have different reliability. For example, a concrete behavior questionnaire or detailed free-text observation can be stronger than an optional “too easy” activity rating. No observation should reduce confidence/staleness, not automatically reduce capability status.

Signal semantics matter: a targeted questionnaire answer of NO may provide negative evidence because the question explicitly probes the Capability. By contrast, the generic Jev free-text classifier only estimates whether an observation positively supports each Capability; a low P(true) is normally absence of positive support, not negative evidence.

Practical runtime

Structured parent answer / free text / optional signal
                    ↓
      direct mapping or Jev classification
                    ↓
  per-Capability evidence/support signal
                    ↓
     belief updater + history + recency in code
                    ↓
             CapabilityBelief
                    ↓
              Learning Frontier

Where BKT can fit later

If a future in-app game produces dense repeated attempts reliably tagged to a Skill, BKT/knowledge tracing can become a specialized evidence source underneath the Capability Belief Model:

Parent sparse evidence ── Jev ──┐
                                ├─→ Capability Belief → Recommendation
Dense tagged app attempts ─ BKT ┘
Current decision
Use Bayesian thinking, not classical BKT as the product’s top-level learner model. The graph is a prior/recommendation structure, not a law; direct evidence can update a Capability even when predecessor Capabilities are unknown.

Jev-Centered AI Architecture

Jev is used as a structured probabilistic classifier, not as the persistent learner model. The simplest generic schema for parent observations is intentionally flat: one independent yes/no (noul) question for each of the 43 Capabilities.

Architecture principle
Jev classifies the current observation. The Capability Belief Model remembers across time. Ordinary code handles graph constraints/history. The recommendation policy decides.

Primary generic schema — 43 flat Capability support questions

Input one parent observation as shared state. Ask all 43 Capability questions at the same level; there is no domain hierarchy in the Jev request.

{
  "state": {
    "child_age": 6,
    "parent_observation": "She said 8+5 is 13 because she made 10 first."
  },
  "questions": {
    "NQ-01": {
      "type": "noul",
      "instructions": "Does this observation provide meaningful positive evidence that the child demonstrates NQ-01 — Quantity as a Set Property? Answer true only when the observation actually supports this capability; otherwise false."
    },
    "...": {},
    "MC-04": {
      "type": "noul",
      "instructions": "Does this observation provide meaningful positive evidence that the child demonstrates MC-04 — Reflection, Review & Consolidation? Answer true only when the observation actually supports this capability; otherwise false."
    }
  }
}

For each Capability, consume the returned probability of true as a simple observation-support percentage:

{
  "NQ-01": 0.03,
  "NQ-02": 0.05,
  "AB-02": 0.91,
  "MT-03": 0.68,
  "MC-02": 0.54
}
Critical interpretation
AB-02 = 0.91 means “this observation strongly supports AB-02.” It does NOT mean “the child has 91% mastery of AB-02.”
Likewise, ME-02 = 0.01 normally means the observation says almost nothing positive about area reasoning; it does not mean the child has only 1% area capability.

Why yes/no instead of four evidence labels

The generic classifier intentionally avoids SUPPORTS / CONTRADICTS / MIXED / INSUFFICIENT. The product does not need that granularity for ad-hoc observation routing, and the simpler output is easier to use, validate and explain.

parent observation
      ↓
43 independent Jev yes/no questions
      ↓
43 P(true) values
      ↓
keep meaningful support signals
      ↓
Capability Belief updater

Question template

Each question should still include the Capability definition and exclusion boundary from the ontology so adjacent constructs remain distinguishable:

type: "noul"

instructions:
  Evaluate ONLY whether the supplied parent observation provides
  meaningful positive evidence about {CAPABILITY_ID} — {NAME}.

  Definition: {FORMAL_DEFINITION}
  Boundary: {BOUNDARY_EXCLUSION}

  Answer true only when information actually present in the observation
  supports this capability. Do not infer it from age, a related capability,
  general intelligence, or lack of contrary evidence.
  If unrelated, vague, or insufficient, answer false.

Structured questionnaire versus generic observation classifier

InputMeaningRecommended handling
Targeted questionnaire: YESPositive evidence for the Capability/Indicator being askedUpdate belief positively according to indicator reliability.
Targeted questionnaire: NOPotential negative evidence because a specific behavior was explicitly probedMay update belief downward, conservatively.
Targeted questionnaire: NOT_SURENo usable observationDo not update mastery; optionally increase uncertainty/staleness.
Free-text Jev P(true) highThe free-text observation positively supports this CapabilityAdd positive evidence weighted by probability/source reliability.
Free-text Jev P(true) lowThe observation does not positively support this CapabilityUsually no belief decrease. Treat as no positive evidence.

Example

For “She said 8+5 is 13 because she made 10 first.”, expected high/medium support should cluster around AB-02 (Additive Relations & Calculation), potentially MT-03 (Structural Noticing & Generalization), and possibly MC-02 (Strategy Awareness & Progress Monitoring), depending on whether the wording reflects the child's own explanation.

Production optimization

The 43-flat-question version is excellent for Playground testing and can remain the reference classifier. In production, cost/latency can later be reduced by cheap deterministic/context filtering or a first-stage relevance pass so only plausible Capability questions are sent. Do this only if measurement shows a benefit; do not complicate the MVP prematurely.

Other Jev uses remain optional

  • Select or score the next 2–4 parent questionnaire questions when uncertainty matters.
  • Classify a learning need such as VERIFY / INTRODUCE / PRACTICE / EXTEND / REVIEW if deterministic rules are insufficient.
  • Score a small top-K set of eligible activities on readiness/variety/effort/preferences.

Validation

Jev output is probabilistic classification, not ground truth. Build a gold-label observation set and compare the 43 support probabilities against expert judgments, especially for adjacent Capabilities. Track false-positive support, false-negative support and calibration before using these probabilities strongly in learner-state updates.

Recommendation Engine — Practical Version

1. Candidate capability frontier in code

Use current status, prerequisite readiness, age as a prior, staleness/review need, uncertainty, downstream importance and recent repetition.

2. Determine learning need

UNKNOWN    → VERIFY
NOT_YET    → INTRODUCE
DEVELOPING → PRACTICE
CONSISTENT + narrow exposure → EXTEND
CONSISTENT + stale           → REVIEW

These are defaults, not rigid rules.

3. Filter activities

Filter by capability mapping, age, materials, duration, parent involvement, online/off-screen mode, accessibility and repetition.

4. AI only where it helps

If many candidates remain, Jev can score top candidates for readiness, variety, effort and child preference; otherwise code is enough.

5. Explain why

“Next we’re working on comparing two-digit numbers because your child appears comfortable with tens and ones, but you’re not yet sure whether they can compare close values without counting one by one.”

Concrete Modeling Examples

Counting

Do not treat “count to 10 / 20 / 50” as separate fundamental capabilities. Distinguish verbal sequence, one-to-one correspondence, cardinality, producing N, numeral recognition, quantity mapping, count-on/back, etc. Use number range, layout, density and manipulability as difficulty parameters.

Sorting / “size”

Separate length, height, area/coverage, capacity, volume and mass. Sorting 3/5/10 objects usually changes task complexity, not the underlying attribute concept.

Addition strategy

COUNT_ALL → COUNT_ON → KNOWN_FACT / DIRECT_RECALL → DECOMPOSITION / MAKE_TEN / FLEXIBLE STRATEGY

Correctness and strategy sophistication remain conceptually separate even if MVP does not yet maintain per-child SkillState.

Structure-discovery family

3 red blocks + 2 red blocks
→ 3 apples + 2 apples
→ 3 children on bus + 2 enter
→ 5 split into 3 and 2
→ 3 + ? = 5
→ "Show me another way to make 5"

Competitive Positioning and Monetization

Competitors already offer adaptive math, skill libraries and games (IXL, SplashLearn, Matific, DreamBox, Prodigy, Khan Academy Kids and others). “AI personalized math” or “fun personalized math games” is not enough.

Defensible differentiation

  • parent-visible developmental map;
  • explainable “why this next activity?”;
  • math thinking + metacognition, not only curriculum content;
  • structure-discovery activities;
  • longitudinal capability data;
  • low parent burden;
  • online + real-world activities.

Business model

Favor parent-paid subscription over advertising. Earlier working hypothesis: test roughly the $80–120/year region for one child, plus family pricing; this remains a hypothesis, not an established optimum.

Freemium

Prefer monetizing depth of insight/personalization rather than blocking learning. Free can include an initial map/basic activities; paid can include continuous personalization, rich explanations, long-term history, multiple children, weekly plans and parent-guided real-world activities.

Conversion trigger

Optimize for the first meaningful or surprising insight, not just trial expiry.
